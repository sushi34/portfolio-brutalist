## The Challenge

StyleHub, a growing multi-vendor fashion marketplace, was struggling with a 2.1% conversion rate — well below the industry average of 3.5%. Users were dropping off at multiple points in the funnel, particularly during product discovery and checkout.

**Key problems identified:**
- Confusing navigation with 47 top-level categories
- Product pages lacking trust signals and social proof
- 5-step checkout process with unnecessary fields
- Mobile experience accounting for 68% of traffic but only 31% of revenue
- No design system leading to inconsistent UI across 200+ pages

## Research & Discovery

### User Research
Conducted 12 user interviews and analyzed 3 months of Hotjar recordings, GA4 data, and customer support tickets.

**Key findings:**
- 73% of users couldn't find the category they wanted within 3 clicks
- Mobile users abandoned cart 2.3x more than desktop
- "Trust" was the #1 reason for hesitation (no reviews visible, unclear returns)
- Power users wanted bulk actions; casual users wanted simplicity

### Competitive Analysis
Analyzed 8 direct competitors and 5 aspirational benchmarks (ASOS, Zalando, Farfetch).

## Strategy & Approach

### Information Architecture Redesign
- Reduced 47 categories → 12 primary + dynamic filters
- Implemented mega-menu with visual category previews
- Added "Shop by Style" and "Shop by Occasion" alternate paths
- Created unified taxonomy for 500+ brands

### Design System Creation
Built a comprehensive design system in Figma with:
- 84 reusable components
- 12 color tokens (light/dark mode ready)
- 8 spacing tokens
- Typography scale with fluid clamping
- Documentation for developers in Zeroheight

### Mobile-First Redesign
Prioritized mobile experience with:
- Bottom navigation for thumb-friendly access
- Swipeable product cards
- One-thumb checkout flow
- Progressive disclosure for advanced filters

## Design Process

### Wireframing & Prototyping
Started with low-fidelity flows for critical paths:
1. Homepage → Product Discovery
2. Product Page → Add to Cart
3. Cart → Checkout → Confirmation

Iterated through 3 rounds of usability testing (5 users each) using Maze for unmoderated tests.

### High-Fidelity Design
Key screens designed:
- **Homepage**: Personalized hero, trending categories, social proof bar
- **Category Pages**: Faceted search, infinite scroll, visual filters
- **Product Page**: Sticky add-to-cart, size guide modal, reviews carousel, trust badges
- **Checkout**: Single-page, guest option, progress indicator, auto-fill
- **Account Dashboard**: Order history, wishlist, returns center

### Design Handoff
- Annotated Figma files with interaction specs
- Component library published to Figma Community
- Design tokens exported as JSON for engineering
- Recorded Loom walkthroughs for complex interactions

## Results

| Metric | Before | After | Change |
|--------|--------|-------|--------|
| Conversion Rate | 2.1% | 3.1% | **+47%** |
| Mobile Conversion | 1.4% | 2.8% | **+100%** |
| Avg. Order Value | $67 | $89 | **+33%** |
| Cart Abandonment | 78% | 62% | **-16pp** |
| Pages per Session | 4.2 | 5.8 | **+38%** |
| Return Rate | 12% | 8% | **-33%** |

**Additional wins:**
- 40% reduction in customer support tickets
- Design system adopted by 3 other teams
- Featured in Figma Community (2.3k likes)
- Client renewed for Year 2 retainer

## Reflections

**What worked:**
- Mobile-first approach paid off disproportionately
- Design system investment saved ~200 hours in Phase 2
- Continuous testing prevented costly rework

**What I'd do differently:**
- Start accessibility audit earlier (WCAG 2.1 AA compliance added 2 weeks)
- Include more quantitative A/B testing in Phase 1
- Better stakeholder alignment on scope creep boundaries
