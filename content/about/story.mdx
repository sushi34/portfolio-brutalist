## The Beginning

I wrote my first line of HTML in 2019. It was a terrible personal website — bright green text on black background, centered everything, not a single semantic tag in sight. But it worked. And that feeling of *making something appear on screen* never left me.

What started as curiosity about "how websites work" became an obsession with "how people use things." I realized pretty quickly that the code was just a means to an end. The real craft was understanding why someone clicks, hesitates, abandons, converts.

## Self-Taught, Community-Taught

I didn't go to design school. I learned from:
- **Books**: Don't Make Me Think, The Design of Everyday Things, Refactoring UI, About Face
- **Courses**: Google UX Certificate, Nielsen Norman Group, Interaction Design Foundation
- **Practice**: 50+ side projects, each one teaching me something the last one didn't
- **Community**: Figma Community, Dribbble, Twitter, Discord servers, Reddit
- **Failure**: Every broken layout, confusing flow, and client rejection taught me more than any success

## First Real Projects

My first paid project was a landing page for a local bakery. $300. They wanted "something modern." I gave them a parallax-scrolling single page with a hamburger menu that didn't work on mobile. They loved it. I cringe now, but that project taught me: **clients don't know what they need — they know what they want. My job is bridging that gap.**

Next came a SaaS dashboard for a friend's startup. Then an e-commerce site for a clothing brand. Then a redesign for a non-profit. Each project forced me to learn something new: payments, authentication, CMS, accessibility, performance, design systems.

## The Agency Years

In 2022, I joined a digital agency. It was trial by fire — 20+ projects in 18 months, spanning industries I'd never touched: fintech, healthcare, logistics, edtech, proptech.

**What the agency taught me:**
- How to present design decisions to stakeholders who don't speak design
- The art of the design handoff (developers are your users too)
- Working in sprints, not waterfalls
- That "good enough" ships, "perfect" doesn't
- How to say no to scope creep without saying no to the client

## Going Independent

Late 2023, I realized I wanted to go deeper, not wider. I wanted to own the entire relationship — strategy through delivery — with clients who valued design as an investment, not an expense.

Since going independent:
- 15+ projects across 8 countries
- Specialized in high-impact work: conversion, clarity, craft
- Built a network of trusted developers, copywriters, strategists
- Still learning something new every project

## What Drives Me

**Empathy as a competitive advantage.** The best interfaces feel inevitable. They anticipate needs before users articulate them. That only happens when you genuinely understand the human on the other side of the screen.

**Clarity over cleverness.** If a user has to think about how to use something, I've failed. The best design is invisible — it just works.

**Results justify the craft.** Beautiful designs that don't convert are just art. I measure success by your metrics: revenue, signups, reduced support tickets, faster task completion.

**Technical fluency makes better designers.** Understanding constraints, possibilities, and implementation details means my designs are buildable, performant, and maintainable. Developers shouldn't have to guess.
